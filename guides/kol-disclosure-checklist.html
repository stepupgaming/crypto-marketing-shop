KOL paid-promotion disclosure checklist

US-oriented ops checklist for people who pay creators. Not legal advice. We are not your lawyer.

If you paid someone to talk about a token, app, or campaign, they need to say they were paid. Hidden ads get campaigns killed and can get you in trouble. This page is a working checklist, not a statute.

Before the post goes live

  • The brief says the post is paid, and the creator agreed in writing (email is fine).
  • The creator knows they must disclose in the post itself, not only in a bio or a later comment.
  • You have a unique tracking link so you can tell their traffic from everyone else.
  • You are not asking them to promise price, returns, or “this will 10x.”

On the post

  • The disclosure is in plain language: paid, sponsored, ad, or a clear #ad that a normal person will see.
  • It is at the start, not buried after a “see more” tap.
  • If the post is a video or Spaces, they say it out loud too.
  • If they have a position in the token, they say that as well. Payment and bag are different facts.

After it ships

  • You saved the live URL, the go-live time, and a screenshot of the disclosure.
  • You logged the fee on the same row as the URL.
  • If they edit the post and strip the disclosure, you write them and keep that email.

Official starting point: the FTC’s endorsement guides. Read those if you are the one paying. This checklist does not replace them.
